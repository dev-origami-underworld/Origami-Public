# THE FREEWARE REVIVAL — A MANIFESTO
### Origami Underworld · Idea Software Studio

*A 1980s-style, $0-infrastructure, zero-hosting-cost experiment in giving games away — for real, right now, to everyone.*

---

## What this is

This is a game you can play by opening a link. No account. No app store. No subscription. No launcher. No "ecosystem." No servers we pay for and that can be switched off. Just a URL and a browser.

It is built the way software was meant to be shared: **freeware.** The 1980s spirit — you made something good, you gave it away, and a kid with a hand-me-down machine got to play it. We are reviving that, deliberately, with modern tools and **\$0 of recurring infrastructure cost**, distributed straight to market through public Git. No middlemen. No toll booths. No gatekeepers.

## Who it's for

**The gaming community — all of it.** And we mean the part that the industry quietly leaves behind:

- **Poor kids,** who can't pay for a console, a subscription, a season pass, or the "free-to-play" game that bleeds money out of their parents.
- **Disabled kids,** especially **blind kids,** who are usually an afterthought — a checkbox, a "someday." Here, blind and sighted players engage the **exact same game**: the same world, narrated and spatialized so it can be *played*, not just *described*.

This is inclusion as the default, not the deluxe tier.

## Why we're doing it this way

Because the promise that *"someday, when it's profitable, we'll do right by these kids"* is lip service, and everyone knows it. We are not waiting for an industry to grow a conscience and decide to do the right thing.

**We are doing it. Now.** No permission, no platform's blessing, no roadmap to "eventually." The implementation is the statement.

> **Our kids are not economic slaves to corrupt software developers.**

A child's right to play, to learn, to be included, is not a revenue opportunity. We refuse to treat it like one.

## How it stays free and unstoppable

- **\$0 infrastructure:** static files, served from a public repository and free CDN-backed pages. Nothing to bill, nothing to shut down.
- **Public Git distribution:** the whole thing is a folder anyone can mirror. You cannot take down what everyone can re-host. If we vanish, the game doesn't.
- **No data harvested, no ads, no dark patterns, no "engagement" traps.** It respects the player because the player is a person, not a metric.
- **Accessible by design (WCAG-class):** keyboard play, screen-reader narration, spatial audio, high-contrast — for the same single game everyone else plays.

## The license, in spirit

Free to play. Free to share. Free to mirror. **Not** free to fence off, rebrand, or sell back to the people we made it for. Attribution and this mission travel with the code. (See `LICENSE`.)

## Dedication

Origami Underworld is a software memoriam created in loving memory of **Mark's mother and father**, whose family heritage was Japanese and American.

Mark's dad loved the three kinds of games folded into this one world: **text adventures, first-person exploration, and top-down games.** The afterlife konbini at the heart of the game, the **7-Eternal**, is dedicated to both of his parents—a warm place where their memory can keep traveling, built for the kids who were never meant to be left at the door.
